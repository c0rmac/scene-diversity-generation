Results

A scene is a connected component of the set of strongly stable (flat) equilibria: two configurations belong to the same scene when a path of equilibria joins them. The solver (Formulation) is PCBO with 1,000 particles that move only by the consensus SDE. When a cluster of particles converges, an intrinsic metric on the stable set (checked paths of equilibria) decides whether its scene lies in a component already reported, and reports it only if not. Over 300 iterations on three seeds it reported 127, 137 and 171 scenes, of which 102, 111 and 130 are distinct components (80.3%, 81.0%, 76.0%; 78.9% pooled). After the first 100 iterations the share holds at about 81% per block of 50 iterations, and the count of distinct scenes keeps growing.

Formulation

The solver that produced the results above, stated in general form. The problem instance (the mug and the spoon) and the values chosen for the run are in Section 10.

The solver is a polarised consensus-based optimiser (PCBO, Fornasier–Sun, arXiv 2403.06662) whose consensus point is refined by a balanced energy-distance functional (Fornasier–Hütter, arXiv 1310.1120): attraction to a target spread over the solutions found, repulsion from the other consensus points and from the scenes already reported, and an adaptive noise floor. Density estimates from MASEM (Braun, Burghoff and Toussaint, arXiv 2605.12338) weight the consensus and set its local scales. An intrinsic metric on the stable set decides which converged consensus points are reported. The output is the set of reported scenes; each should be a different connected component of the stable set.

1. Spaces

A configuration of rigid bodies $B \in \mathcal{B}$ resting on a horizontal support under gravity is

$$ x = (p_B, q_B)_{B \in \mathcal{B}} \in X = (\mathbb{R}^3 \times S^3)^{\mathcal{B}} , $$

$p_B$ a position and $q_B$ a unit quaternion ($\pm q$ identified). Gravity and the support are invariant under $G = SE(2)$ (horizontal translations, rotations about the vertical), acting on all bodies at once; the physically distinct configurations form $X/G$, of dimension $6|\mathcal{B}| - 3$.

Motions. An infinitesimal motion is a twist $u = (v_B, L\,\omega_B)_{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{6|\mathcal{B}|}$, $v_B$ the velocity of the body's origin, $\omega_B$ its angular velocity and $L$ a length per radian. The gauge directions (the Lie algebra of $G$) are removed from every tangent space below.

Gauge-free distance. For $a, b \in X$ let $g_{ab} \in G$ align $b$ to $a$ (the rotation about the vertical that best aligns a reference body, then the translation that matches its horizontal position). Then

$$ d(a,b) = \Big( \sum_{B} \|p_B^a - p_B^{g_{ab} b}\|^2 + L^2\, \theta(q_B^a, q_B^{g_{ab} b})^2 \Big)^{1/2}, \qquad \theta(q,q') = 2\arccos|\langle q, q'\rangle| . $$

2. Contact strata

Every configuration the solver evaluates lies on a contact stratum. The strata enter the landing map, the flatness test, the metric, the repulsion weights and the path checks.

Constraint functions. Features $g_j : X \to \mathbb{R}$, $j = 1,\dots,F$, each $\ge 0$ off contact and $0$ in contact: signed distances between pairs of geometric parts (body to support, body to body), and point gaps that sample a part's faces and edges so that a face lying flush is several point gaps at $0$.

Active and held features. Feature $j$ is active at $x$ when $g_j(x) \le b$, for a contact band $b$. The held set $H(x)$ is the active set less every pair gap that equals the least active point gap of the same pair (the pair is then in a face or line contact, which its point gaps imply).

Stratum. For a set $H$ of features,

$$ \Sigma_H = \{x \in X :\ g_j(x) = 0 \ (j \in H),\ \ g_j(x) > 0 \ (j \notin H)\}, $$

locally a manifold with tangent space

$$ T_x\Sigma_H = \{u :\ \nabla g_j(x)\cdot u = 0\ (j \in H),\ u \perp \text{gauge}\}, \qquad \dim(\Sigma_H/G) = 6|\mathcal{B}| - 3 - r_H(x), $$

$r_H(x)$ the rank of the held gradients with the gauge removed (each gradient scaled to unit length; from the gaps' witness points where available, else central differences). The strata partition the configurations by contact pattern; a connected component of stable configurations may cross several strata, making or breaking a contact along a path.

Projection and stratum steps. $\text{proj}_{\Sigma_H}(x)$ is damped Gauss–Newton with minimum-norm steps on a maximal independent subset of $H$, chosen greedily on the unit gradients (a row counts when its component outside the span of the earlier rows exceeds $\theta_p$), then a check that the dependent features hold too, else the full set. A stratum step of length $\rho$ along a unit $u \in T_x\Sigma_H$ is $x \mapsto \text{proj}_{\Sigma_H}(x \oplus \rho u)$.

Where the strata enter.

  1. The landing map (Section 4) projects every evaluation onto a stratum of a target rank.
  2. The flatness test (Section 3) takes random stratum steps.
  3. A particle that was flat, is still exact and holds the same set $H$ is in the same flat family, and its flatness test is not repeated.
  4. The kernel metric (Section 5.1) has the contact term $\lambda_c^2 |H_a \triangle H_b|$.
  5. The repulsion weights (Section 6.3) are divided by one plus the number of stratum transitions beyond one along the path between two clusters.
  6. The component metric's path checks (Section 5.4) project segments onto the contacts held at both ends; its planner slides within the held contacts or releases one at a time.

3. Equilibria and the flat set

At a configuration with active contacts $c = 1,\dots,n_c$ (normals $n_c$, friction coefficient $\mu$), each circular friction cone is replaced by the inscribed polygonal cone with $K_c$ generators $f_{c,k}$. The equilibrium residual is

$$ V_{\text{in}}(x) = \min_{\lambda \ge 0}\ \Big\| \sum_{c,k} \lambda_{c,k}\, W_{c,k}(x) - w_g \Big\| , $$

$W_{c,k}$ the wrench of $f_{c,k}$ on all bodies and $w_g$ the wrench balancing gravity (non-negative least squares with a KKT check). Because the cones are inscribed, $V_{\text{in}} = 0$ certifies an equilibrium. A configuration is valid when no gap is below $-\epsilon_{\text{pen}}$ and the bodies are in mutual contact.

  • Exact: valid and $V_{\text{in}}(x) \le \epsilon_{\text{eq}}$.
  • Flat (robust): exact, and at least half of $K_f$ random stratum steps of length $\rho_f$ stay exact (tested sequentially, stopping as soon as the majority is decided).

A scene is a connected component of $\mathcal{F}_{\text{flat}}/G$, the flat configurations up to the support's symmetry.

4. Search space, landing map, objective

PCBO moves in the input space $Z$ of a landing law: one orientation per body, and positions and heights in boxes (the reference body's horizontal position fixed, which removes the translational gauge), with reference law $\mu_0$ uniform (Haar on each orientation). Steps use chart coordinates $z' \ominus z \in \mathbb{R}^{\dim Z}$ (rotation vectors and coordinate differences, each normalised by its range) and $z \oplus s$ (left multiplication by the exponential on each orientation, reflection at the box boundaries).

Landing map $\Lambda_r : Z \to X \cup \{\varnothing\}$ for a target rank $r$: form the pose $x_0(z)$; hold features in increasing order of gap while each raises the rank of the held gradients with the gauge (a row counts when its component outside the span exceeds $\theta_r$), up to rank $r$, every penetrating feature included; project onto $\Sigma_H$; add new penetrations and project again, up to $n_{\text{proj}}$ rounds; otherwise the landing fails. $\Lambda_r$ is piecewise smooth and many-to-one. Particle $i$ keeps a fixed target rank $r_i$ (the ranks cycle over the particles); $x_i = \Lambda_{r_i}(z_i)$ is its image, the unlanded pose if the landing failed.

Objective. $E(z) = \min\{V_{\text{in}}(\Lambda(z)),\,E_{\max}\}$ for a valid landing, $E = E_{\max}$ otherwise. Every flat state is a global minimiser. One oracle call evaluates a landing; the flatness test adds a few.

5. The metrics

5.1 Placement metric with a contact term

$$ d_X(a,b)^2 = \sum_B \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|} \Big(\lvert c^a_B - c^b_B\rvert^2 + \operatorname{tr}\big((R^a_B - R^b_B)\,\Sigma_B\,(R^a_B - R^b_B)^\top\big)\Big), \qquad d_G(a,b) = \min_{g\in G} d_X(a, g b), $$

the root-mean-square displacement of each body's matter ($c_B$ the centre of mass, $\Sigma_B$ the second moment), the support's symmetry divided out by a closed-form planar Procrustes step; $d_G$ is a metric on scenes. With the contact term,

$$ \delta(a,b)^2 = d_G(a,b)^2 + \lambda_c^2\,\lvert H_a \,\triangle\, H_b\rvert , $$

$\lambda_c$ the bodies' mean RMS radius, so gaining or losing a contact counts as much as moving a body by its own size.

5.2 Kernel distance

On the images of the swarm, the $k_g$-nearest-neighbour graph with edge lengths $\delta(x_i,x_j)/s$; $D_{ij}$ is its shortest-path distance (Bernstein–de Silva–Langford–Tenenbaum). $D$ is the distance in the consensus kernel.

5.3 MASEM's nearest-neighbour estimates

The solver estimates where it has been from its own flat arrivals. Following MASEM, which weights samples of a constraint manifold by a power of their distance to the $k$-th nearest sample, let $A_t$ be the flat arrivals up to iteration $t$ (the configurations at which a particle became flat; a particle that stays flat adds none), $n_t = |A_t|$, and

$$ k_t = \lceil \sqrt{n_t}\, \rceil, \qquad \varepsilon_t(x) = \text{the } k_t\text{-th smallest of } \{ d_G(x,a) : a \in A_t,\ d_G(x,a) > 0\} . $$

As $k_t \to \infty$ with $k_t/n_t \to 0$, the nearest-neighbour density estimate on the $p$-dimensional flat set,

$$ \hat\rho_t(x) = \frac{k_t}{n_t\, \omega_p\, \varepsilon_t(x)^{p}} \qquad (\omega_p \text{ the volume of the unit } p\text{-ball}), $$

is consistent (Loftsgaarden–Quesenberry). Two scales are measured from the run at every iteration, on a random subsample $S_t \subseteq A_t$, with $T_j(a)$ the distance from $a$ to its $j$-th nearest other arrival:

$$ r_t = \operatorname{median}_{a \in S_t} \varepsilon_t(a), \qquad p_t = \Big( \frac{1}{|S_t|} \sum_{a\in S_t} \frac{1}{k_t - 1} \sum_{j=1}^{k_t-1} \log \frac{T_{k_t}(a)}{T_j(a)} \Big)^{-1} , $$

the median radius and the intrinsic dimension (the Levina–Bickel maximum-likelihood estimate, its inverse averaged over points as MacKay and Ghahramani propose). The visit potential compares the density at $x$ with the run's typical density,

$$ V_t(x) = \log\frac{\hat\rho_t(x)}{\hat\rho_t\text{ at radius } r_t} = p_t \log \frac{r_t}{\varepsilon_t(x)} , $$

positive where the run has been more often than typical. The same nearest-neighbour rule classifies the flat set: with $\mathcal{V}_t$ the run's evaluations so far (each flat or not) and $\kappa_t = \lceil\sqrt{|\mathcal{V}_t|}\,\rceil$,

$$ \phi_t(z) = \frac{1}{\kappa_t}\, \#\{\text{flat states among the } \kappa_t \text{ evaluations nearest } z \text{ in } d_G\} . $$

These estimates are used four times: the consensus weights carry $e^{-V_t/2} = (\varepsilon_t/r_t)^{p_t/2}$, MASEM's distance-power weight with exponent $p_t/2$ (Section 6.1); the noise floor is proportional to $\varepsilon_t$ (Section 7); $\varepsilon_t$ is each reported scene's radius in the basin pull-back, and $\phi_t$ along a path gives the repulsion's affinity (Section 6.3).

5.4 The component metric

$$ d_\Sigma(a,b) = \inf\{\text{length}(\gamma) : \gamma \text{ a path in } \mathcal{F}_{\text{flat}} \text{ from } a \text{ to } b\}, \qquad d_\Sigma = \infty \text{ across components}. $$

The solver estimates whether $d_\Sigma(a,b) < \infty$ by a checked join. A segment is exact when its interior points, spaced at most $h$ apart and projected onto the contacts held at both ends, are all exact. A join of $a$ to $b$ is the straight segment when $d(a,b) \le R_s$; otherwise a directed planner whose steps (at most $H_p$ long) go towards $b$ along the tangent space of the contacts kept (sliding), or release one contact that $b$ does not have, a step being accepted when the pose is exact and the segment from the previous pose is exact; the planner gives up after $n_{\text{stall}}$ steps without progress. A successful join shows $d_\Sigma(a,b) < \infty$ at resolution $h$; a failed join shows nothing. Precision requires $h$ below the narrowest gap between components.

6. Consensus points

6.1 Weights

Particle $i$ weights every particle $j$ by

$$ \omega_{ij} \propto \exp\big(-\alpha_i D_{ij}^2\big)\, e^{-V_t(x_j)/2} , $$

the polarised locality kernel times MASEM's tilt towards rarely visited flat states. $\alpha_i$ is chosen by bisection so that particle $i$'s consensus measure has effective sample size $(\sum_j \omega_{ij})^2 / \sum_j \omega_{ij}^2 = n_{\text{eff}}$. The weights use images only; the objective does not enter them.

6.2 Continuous consensus point

$$ m_i = z_i \oplus \sum_j \omega_{ij}\, (z_j \ominus z_i) . $$

6.3 Repelled consensus points (balanced energy distance)

Starting from $c_i = m_i$, the consensus points take $n_B$ Jacobi gradient steps of size $\eta$ (each coordinate clipped at $c_B$) on

$$ \mathcal{B}_i(c) = |c \ominus m_i|^2 + \mu_t \Big[ \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\omega_t}|c \ominus y|^{q} - \frac{1}{|N_i|}\sum_{j \in N_i} a_{ij}\,|c \ominus c_j|^{q} - \frac{1}{K_b}\sum_{y \in P_t(c)} |c \ominus y|^{q} \Big] , $$

the energy distance with kernel $|\cdot|^q$, $1 \le q < 2$, made up of:

  • Target $\omega_t$: points on segments between inputs that produced flat landings, the inputs drawn with weight proportional to a power $m_\omega$ of the distance of their image to the nearest other flat image, so the target spreads evenly over the solutions found rather than following how often each was found.
  • Repulsion among consensus points: $N_i$ are the $K_r$ consensus points nearest $c_i$ whose cluster has another representative flat state (the flat state of the cluster's consensus support nearest its consensus point). Each is weighted by the affinity that the two clusters share a component,
    $$ a_{ij} = \frac{\min_{s = 1,\dots,9} \phi_t\big(x^{\text{rep}}_i + \tfrac{s}{10}(x^{\text{rep}}_j - x^{\text{rep}}_i)\big)}{1 + \max(0,\ \sigma_{ij} - 1)} , $$
    $\sigma_{ij}$ the number of stratum transitions between the two representatives: likely-same clusters push apart, likely-different ones barely interact.
  • Basin pull-back $P_t(c)$: the $K_b$ nearest to $c$ of the inputs of flat arrivals whose image lies within $\varepsilon_t(y)$ of a reported scene $y$ (the reported scenes pulled back through the landing map).
  • Weight $\mu_t = \beta\, \operatorname{median}_{i\ne j} |m_i \ominus m_j|^{2-q}$, scale-free in the swarm's spread.

Write $\ell_i = |c_i \ominus z_i|$ and $u_i = (c_i \ominus z_i)/\ell_i$.

7. The dynamics

$$ dZ^i_t = \lambda\, \ell_i\, u_i\, dt \;+\; \sigma\, \operatorname{diag}\!\big(|\ell_i u_{i,k}| + \kappa_i\big)_{k}\, dB^i_t , \qquad X^i_t = \Lambda_{r_i}(Z^i_t), $$

drift towards the particle's own repelled consensus point and anisotropic noise (Carrillo et al. 2019) with a local floor

$$ \kappa_i = \frac{\varepsilon_t(x_i)}{s_z} \sqrt{\frac{1-\rho}{\dim Z\; \sigma^2 \Delta t}}, \qquad \rho = (1-\lambda\Delta t)^2 + \sigma^2\Delta t , $$

chosen so that a consensus measure's stationary spread equals the local spacing of the flat arrivals around the particle's image ($\varepsilon_t(x_i)$ at a flat image, $r_t$ otherwise; $s_z$ converts lengths to input units). Euler–Maruyama with step $\Delta t$, $\xi \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, each coordinate clipped at $c_s$:

$$ s_i = \lambda \Delta t\, \ell_i u_i + \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}\, \big(|\ell_i u_{i,k}| + \kappa_i\big)_k \odot \xi, \qquad z_i \leftarrow z_i \oplus \operatorname{clip}(s_i) . $$

The particles move only by this SDE.

8. Convergence and the report

Converged particles. The spread of particle $i$'s consensus measure is $V_i = \sum_j \omega_{ij}\,|z_j \ominus c_i|^2$. With the relaxation time $\tau = \lceil 1/(-\ln\rho)\rceil$, particle $i$ has stalled when the mean of $V_i$ over the last $\tau$ steps is not below its mean over the $\tau$ steps before.

Clusters. Stalled particles $a, b$ are linked when $|c_a \ominus c_b|^2 \le \max(V_a, V_b)$ (single linkage). A cluster's representative is the flat particle among its members and their consensus support whose input is nearest the cluster's mean consensus point; its image is the cluster's converged scene. The members' spread history is then cleared, so the cluster may converge again later; the particles stay where they are.

The report. The converged scene is joined (Section 5.4) to its $K_m$ nearest earlier converged scenes within distance $R_m$, with $A_m$ attempts each in alternating directions. If a join succeeds the scene lies in the component of an earlier scene and is not reported; otherwise it is reported. Every converged scene, reported or not, enters the basin pull-back of Section 6.3.

9. One iteration

for each particle i (in parallel):
    land z_i at rank r_i; if valid: one oracle call -> E_i;
        if exact and not in its flat family: sequential flatness test
record the flat arrivals; update k_t, eps_t, r_t, p_t
graph: k_g-NN on images with the contact-term metric, shortest paths D
weights omega_ij ~ exp(-alpha_i D_ij^2) exp(-V_t(x_j)/2), alpha_i by the effective sample size
m_i <- z_i (+) sum_j omega_ij (z_j (-) z_i)

10. The instance and the run

Instance. Two bodies, a mug and a spoon, on a table, friction coefficient $\mu = 10$. The mug's wall is 24 boxes, with a base and a handle of capsules. The $F = 413$ features are the signed distances between the table and every mug and spoon geom and between every mug geom and both spoon geoms, plus point gaps: the 8 corners of every wall box and 8 points of the base's bottom rim against the table, both ends of every handle capsule against the table, and both ends of the spoon's shaft against the table, every wall box, the base and every handle capsule. The input space is $Z = SO(3) \times [0, 0.02\,\text{m}] \times SO(3) \times [-0.2, 0.2]^2 \times [0, 0.2]\,\text{m}$: the mug's orientation, the height of its lowest point, the spoon's orientation and origin, the mug's horizontal position at the origin. Chart coordinates divide rotation vectors by $\pi$, the height by $0.02\,$m and the spoon's position by $0.4\,$m. Equilibrium wrenches are in units of $0.3 \cdot 9.81\,$N and $0.1\,$m. Gaps between neighbouring components can be as narrow as $20$-$100\,\mu$m.

11. How the results are scored

The score is a diagnostic made after the run; the solver never sees it. Each run's converged scenes, reported or not, are merged into a reference of about 21,400 flat states sampled independently of the solver (10,386 flat landings of the landing law and Majid's stable poses), already grouped into components by the same checked joins at $h = 0.25\,$mm and used only as stepping stones. The first merge pass gives every converged scene a component label. A reported scene is a first find when no earlier reported scene has its label; the distinct share is first finds over reported scenes. A component is lost when the run converged in it but every scene there was judged the same as an earlier one. Two scenes in different components are separated at the planner's resolution; separation is not proved.

12. Open points

  • Giant components. Three components hold about 70% of the reference's flat states. Most of the remaining repeats fall in them: their converged scenes lie far apart, beyond the report's range $R_m$. Joins through a denser set of flat states (the solver's own landing-law samples, joined over several passes) connect them.
  • The swarm is mostly off the flat set. The local floor sets a stationary spread equal to the spacing of the flat arrivals, which is wide compared with the thin input regions that land flat. Over 300 iterations only 8-11% of the flat particles are still flat after a step, the noise is about four times the drift, and the consensus spread changes by a factor of about 0.99 per step rather than contracting at $\rho$. Clusters converge as measures over many basins, and the reported scene is the flat landing nearest their consensus point.
  • The component metric is used at the report only. It decides which converged scenes are reported; it does not enter the consensus kernel, so a consensus measure can still couple particles of different components.
  • Lost components. About 10% of the components the swarm converged in were judged the same as an earlier scene and never reported. Confirming each deciding join at a spacing below the narrowest gaps would remove false joins at small cost.
  • Separation. Two scenes in different components are separated at the planner's resolution; no certificate proves them separate.
  • Model. With $\mu = 10$ the friction cones have a half-angle of about 84°, and the mug's wall is 24 flat boxes, so a few equilibria (a spoon held by its shaft tip in a crease between two wall facets) exist only in the model.
