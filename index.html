Results

A scene is a connected component of the set of strongly stable (flat) equilibria: two configurations belong to the same scene when a path of equilibria joins them. The solver (Formulation) is PCBO with 1,000 particles that move only by the consensus SDE. When a cluster of particles converges, an intrinsic metric on the stable set (checked paths of equilibria) decides whether its scene lies in a component already reported, and reports it only if not. Over 300 iterations on three seeds it reported 127, 137 and 171 scenes, of which 102, 111 and 130 are distinct components (80.3%, 81.0%, 76.0%; 78.9% pooled). After the first 100 iterations the share holds at about 81% per block of 50 iterations, and the count of distinct scenes keeps growing.

Formulation

The solver is a polarised consensus-based optimiser (PCBO, Fornasier–Sun, arXiv 2403.06662) with a continuous consensus point refined by an energy-distance functional (Fornasier–Hütter, arXiv 1310.1120): attraction to a target spread over the found solutions, repulsion from the other consensus points and from what has been reported, and an adaptive noise floor. An intrinsic metric on the stable set decides which converged consensus points are reported. Its output is the set of reported scenes; each should be a different connected component of the stable set. Parameter names in typewriter are environment variables of pcbo3d.

The problem is stated generically: rigid bodies, finitely many contact constraints, a static-equilibrium residual. The mug and spoon is the instance.

1. Spaces

A configuration is a pair of rigid poses, the mug $M$ and the spoon $S$:

$$ x = (p_M, q_M, p_S, q_S) \in X = (\mathbb{R}^3 \times S^3)^2 , $$

with $p$ positions and $q$ unit quaternions ($\pm q$ identified). Gravity and the table are invariant under the planar Euclidean group $G = SE(2)$ (translations in $(x,y)$, rotations about $e_z$), acting on both bodies at once; the physically distinct configurations form $X/G$, of dimension $12 - 3 = 9$.

Motions. An infinitesimal motion is a twist $u = (v_M, L\,\omega_M, v_S, L\,\omega_S) \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$ ($v$ the velocity of a body's origin, $\omega$ its angular velocity, $L = 0.1\,$m so every component is a length). The three gauge directions (horizontal translation, rotation about the vertical) are removed from every tangent space below.

Gauge-free distance. For $a, b \in X$ let $g_{ab} \in G$ align $b$ to $a$ (rotate about $e_z$ by the yaw that best aligns the mugs, translate so the mugs' horizontal positions coincide). Then

$$ d(a,b) = \Big( \sum_{B \in \{M,S\}} \|p_B^a - p_B^{g_{ab} b}\|^2 + L^2\, \theta(q_B^a, q_B^{g_{ab} b})^2 \Big)^{1/2}, \qquad \theta(q,q') = 2\arccos|\langle q, q'\rangle| \quad (\texttt{gdist}). $$

2. Contact strata

The solver uses stratification throughout: every evaluated configuration lies on a contact stratum, and the strata enter the landing map, the flatness test, the metric, the repulsion weights and the path checks (listed at the end of this section).

Constraint functions. There are $F = 413$ features $g_j : X \to \mathbb{R}$, each $\ge 0$ off contact and $0$ in contact:

  • geom-pair gaps: the signed distance between the table and every mug and spoon geom, and between every mug geom and both spoon geoms (exact formulas for the curved pairs);
  • point gaps: the 8 corners of each of the mug's 24 wall boxes and 8 points of the base's bottom rim against the table; both ends of every handle capsule against the table; both ends of the spoon's shaft (spheres of the shaft's radius) against the table, every wall box, the base and every handle capsule. A face lying flush is several point gaps at $0$.

Active and held features. Feature $j$ is active at $x$ when $g_j(x) \le b$, $b = 2\cdot 10^{-5}\,$m. The held set $H(x)$ is the active set less every geom-pair gap that equals the least active point gap of the same pair (that pair is in a face or line contact, and its point gaps imply it).

Stratum. For a set $H$ of features,

$$ \Sigma_H = \{x \in X :\ g_j(x) = 0 \ (j \in H),\ \ g_j(x) > 0 \ (j \notin H)\}, $$

locally a manifold whose tangent space at $x$ is

$$ T_x\Sigma_H = \{u \in \mathbb{R}^{12} :\ \nabla g_j(x)\cdot u = 0\ (j \in H),\ u \perp \text{gauge}\}, \qquad \dim(\Sigma_H/G) = 9 - r_H(x), $$

$r_H(x)$ the rank of the held gradients with the gauge directions removed (each gradient scaled to unit length; computed from the gaps' witness points, the closest points and normal, and by central differences where no witness exists). The strata partition the configurations by contact pattern; a connected component of stable configurations may cross several strata (a contact made or broken along a path).

Projection onto a stratum. $\text{proj}_{\Sigma_H}(x)$: damped Gauss–Newton with minimum-norm steps on a maximal independent subset of $H$ (greedy on the unit gradients, a row counting when its component outside the span of the earlier rows exceeds $10^{-3}$), then a check that the dependent features also hold, else the full set. A stratum step of length $\rho$ along a unit $u \in T_x\Sigma_H$ is $x \mapsto \text{proj}_{\Sigma_H}(x \oplus \rho u)$.

Where the strata enter the solver.

  1. Landing map (Section 4): every evaluation projects onto a stratum $\Sigma_H$ of a target rank.
  2. Flatness test (Section 3): random stratum steps.
  3. Flat family: a particle that was flat, is still exact and holds the same set $H$ is in the same flat family and its flatness test is not repeated (LEAN_FLAT_ONCE=1).
  4. Kernel metric (Section 5.1): the contact term $\lambda^2 |H_a \triangle H_b|$.
  5. Repulsion weights (Section 6.3): divided by one plus the number of stratum transitions beyond one along the straight path between two clusters (AFF_REPEL=2).
  6. Path checks of the component metric (Section 5.3): segments projected onto the contacts held at both ends; the planner slides within the held contacts or releases one at a time.

3. Equilibria and the flat set

At a configuration with active contacts $c = 1,\dots,n_c$ (normal $n_c$, friction coefficient $\mu = 10$), the circular friction cone is replaced by the inscribed polygonal cone with $16$ generators $f_{c,k}$. The equilibrium residual is

$$ V_{\text{in}}(x) = \min_{\lambda \ge 0}\ \Big\| \sum_{c,k} \lambda_{c,k}\, W_{c,k}(x) - w_g \Big\| , $$

$W_{c,k} \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$ the wrench of $f_{c,k}$ on both bodies, $w_g$ the wrench balancing gravity (units $0.3 \cdot 9.81\,$N and $0.1\,$m), by Lawson–Hanson NNLS with a KKT check. Because the cone is inscribed, $V_{\text{in}} = 0$ certifies an equilibrium. A configuration is valid when no gap is below $-2\cdot 10^{-7}\,$m and at least one active contact joins mug and spoon.

  • Exact: valid and $V_{\text{in}}(x) \le 10^{-10}$.
  • Flat (robust): exact, and at least half of $8$ random stratum steps of length $\rho = 10^{-5}$ stay exact; tested sequentially (stop at 4 exact or 5 failed steps, the same verdict as the full test).

A scene is a connected component of $\mathcal{F}_{\text{flat}}/G$, the flat configurations up to the table's symmetry. The flat landings have intrinsic dimension about 3-4, matching strata of dimension $9 - r$ for $r = 5,\dots,8$.

4. Search space, landing map, objective

PCBO moves in the input space of the landing law,

$$ Z = SO(3) \times [0, \bar h] \times SO(3) \times B, \qquad \bar h = 0.02\,\text{m}, \quad B = [-0.2, 0.2]^2 \times [0, 0.2]\ \text{m}, $$

$z = (q_M, h, q_S, p_S)$: the mug's orientation, its lowest point at height $h$ above the table, the spoon's orientation and origin (the mug's horizontal position is the origin). Steps use normalised chart coordinates $z' \ominus z = (\tfrac1\pi\log(q_M' q_M^{-1}), \tfrac{h'-h}{\bar h}, \tfrac1\pi\log(q_S' q_S^{-1}), \tfrac{p_S'-p_S}{0.4}) \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$ and $z \oplus s$ (left multiplication by $\exp(\pi s_{\text{rot}})$, reflection at the boundaries).

Landing map $\Lambda_r : Z \to X \cup \{\varnothing\}$, target rank $r \in \{5,6,7,8\}$: form the initial pose $x_0(z)$; hold features in increasing order of gap while each raises the rank of the held gradients with the gauge (a row counts when its component outside the span exceeds $0.05$), up to rank $r$, every penetrating feature included; project onto $\Sigma_H$; add new penetrations and project again, up to 4 rounds; otherwise the landing fails. $\Lambda_r$ is piecewise smooth and many-to-one. Particle $i$ keeps the rank $r_i = 5 + (i \bmod 4)$; $x_i = \Lambda_{r_i}(z_i)$ is its image (the unlanded pose if the landing failed).

Objective. $E(z) = \min\{V_{\text{in}}(\Lambda(z)),\,2\}$ for a valid landing, $E = 2$ for a failed, penetrating or contact-free one. Every flat state is a global minimiser ($E = 0$); one oracle call evaluates a landing, and the flatness test adds a few.

5. The metrics

5.1 Placement metric with a contact term

$$ d_X(a,b)^2 = \sum_B \tfrac1n \Big(\lvert c^a_B - c^b_B\rvert^2 + \operatorname{tr}\big((R^a_B - R^b_B)\,\Sigma_B\,(R^a_B - R^b_B)^\top\big)\Big), \qquad d_G(a,b) = \min_{g\in G} d_X(a, g b), $$

the root-mean-square displacement of each body's matter ($c_B$ the centre of mass, $\Sigma_B$ the second moment), the table's symmetry divided out by a closed-form planar Procrustes step. With the contact term (AX_STRATA=1),

$$ \delta(a,b)^2 = d_G(a,b)^2 + \lambda^2\,\lvert H_a \,\triangle\, H_b\rvert, \qquad \lambda = 4.66\ \text{cm (the bodies' mean RMS radius)} , $$

a metric on scenes: gaining or losing a contact counts as much as moving a body by its own size.

5.2 Kernel distance

On the images of the swarm, the $k$-nearest-neighbour graph ($k = 8$) with edge lengths $\delta(x_i,x_j)/s$, $s = 4.65\,$cm; $D_{ij}$ is its shortest-path distance (Bernstein–de Silva–Langford–Tenenbaum). $D$ is the distance in the consensus kernel.

5.3 The component metric (intrinsic distance on the flat set)

$$ d_\Sigma(a,b) = \inf\{\text{length}(\gamma) : \gamma \text{ a path in } \mathcal{F}_{\text{flat}} \text{ from } a \text{ to } b\}, \qquad d_\Sigma = \infty \text{ across components}. $$

The solver estimates whether $d_\Sigma(a,b) < \infty$ by a checked join (Joiner): a segment is exact when its interior points, spaced at most $h = 0.25\,$mm apart and projected onto the contacts held at both ends, are all exact. A join of $a$ to $b$ is the straight segment when they are within $5\,$cm; otherwise a directed planner whose steps go towards $b$ along the tangent space of the contacts kept (sliding), or release one contact $b$ does not have, accepting a step when the pose is exact and the segment from the previous pose is exact, giving up after 20 steps without progress. A successful join proves $d_\Sigma < \infty$ at resolution $h$; a failed one proves nothing. Precision requires $h$ below the narrowest gap between components; gaps of $20$-$100\,\mu$m occur.

6. Consensus points

6.1 Weights

Particle $i$ weights particle $j$ by

$$ \omega_{ij} \propto \exp\big(-\alpha_i D_{ij}^2\big)\,\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_k(x_j)}{r_t}\Big)^{p/2} , $$

over all particles $j$, with $\varepsilon_k(x)$ the distance to the $k$-th nearest flat arrival of the run ($k = \lceil\sqrt n\rceil$, $n$ the arrivals so far), $r_t$ its median over the arrivals and $p$ their intrinsic dimension (Levina–Bickel), all measured from the run at every iteration. The second factor is the density correction of MASEM (Braun, Burghoff and Toussaint, arXiv 2605.12338): rarely visited flat states weigh more (CBO_V=1). $\alpha_i$ is set by bisection so that each consensus measure has effective sample size $62$ ($=8N/128$, ESS=62). The kernel uses images only: the objective does not enter the weights.

6.2 Continuous consensus point

$$ m_i = z_i \oplus \sum_j \omega_{ij}\, (z_j \ominus z_i) \qquad (\texttt{CONS\_CONT=1}). $$

6.3 Repelled consensus points (balanced energy distance)

The consensus points $c_i$ decrease, by 20 Jacobi gradient steps of size $\eta = 0.05$ (each coordinate clipped at $0.5$), starting from $c_i = m_i$,

$$ \mathcal{B}_i(c) = |c \ominus m_i|^2 + \mu_t \Big[ \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\omega_t}|c \ominus y| - \frac{1}{|N_i|}\sum_{j \in N_i} a_{ij}\,|c \ominus c_j| - \frac{1}{K}\sum_{y \in P_K(c)} |c \ominus y| \Big] , $$
  • Target $\omega_t$: points on segments between inputs that produced flat landings, drawn with weight (distance of their image to the nearest other flat image)$^2$, so the target is spread evenly over the found solutions rather than following how often each was found (AX_TARGET=1).
  • Repulsion among consensus points: $N_i$ are the 8 consensus points nearest $c_i$ (in inputs) whose cluster has another representative flat state, each weighted by the affinity $a_{ij} \in [0,1]$ that the two clusters share a component: along the straight path between the clusters' representative flat states, the least share of flat evaluations among the $\lceil\sqrt N\rceil$ nearest evaluations at 9 interior points, divided by one plus the stratum transitions beyond one (AFF_REPEL=2); likely-same clusters push apart, likely-different ones barely interact.
  • Basin pull-back $P_K(c)$: the $K = 8$ nearest of the inputs of flat arrivals whose image lies within a representative's $k$-NN radius $\varepsilon_k$ (the pull-back of the reported scenes through the landing map; BP=1).
  • Weight $\mu_t = \beta\, \operatorname{median}_{i\ne j} |m_i \ominus m_j|$, $\beta = 5$ (BF_MU=5 BF_SCALE=1), with the energy-distance kernel $|\cdot|^p$, $p = 1$.

Write $\ell_i = |c_i \ominus z_i|$ and $u_i = (c_i \ominus z_i)/\ell_i$.

7. The dynamics

$$ dZ^i_t = \lambda\, \ell_i\, u_i\, dt \;+\; \sigma\, \operatorname{diag}\!\big(|\ell_i u_{i,k}| + \kappa_i\big)_{k=1}^{10}\, dB^i_t , \qquad X^i_t = \Lambda_{r_i}(Z^i_t), $$

$\lambda = 1$, $\sigma = 0.2$: drift towards the particle's own repelled consensus point, anisotropic noise (Carrillo et al. 2019) plus a floor. The floor is local (FLOOR_LOCAL=1):

$$ \kappa_i = \frac{\varepsilon_k(x_i)}{0.2\,\text{m}} \sqrt{\frac{1-\rho}{10\,\sigma^2 \Delta t}}, \qquad \rho = (1-\lambda\Delta t)^2 + \sigma^2\Delta t = 0.7285 , $$

so that a consensus measure's stationary spread equals the local spacing of the run's flat arrivals around the particle's image ($\varepsilon_k$ at a flat image, its median $r_t$ otherwise). Euler–Maruyama with $\Delta t = 0.15$, $\xi \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_{10})$:

$$ s_i = \lambda \Delta t\, \ell_i u_i + \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}\, \big(|\ell_i u_{i,k}| + \kappa_i\big)_k \odot \xi, \qquad s_i \leftarrow \operatorname{clip}_{[-0.5,0.5]}(s_i), \qquad z_i \leftarrow z_i \oplus s_i . $$

The particles move only by this SDE.

8. Convergence and the report

Converged particles. The spread of particle $i$'s consensus measure is $V_i = \sum_j \omega_{ij}\,|z_j \ominus c_i|^2$. Over the relaxation time $\tau = \lceil 1/(-\ln\rho)\rceil = 4$, particle $i$ has stalled when the mean of $V_i$ over the last $\tau$ steps is not below its mean over the $\tau$ steps before.

Clusters. Stalled particles $a, b$ are linked when $|c_a \ominus c_b|^2 \le \max(V_a, V_b)$ (single linkage). A cluster's representative is the flat particle among its members and their consensus support ($\omega_{ij} > 10^{-6}$) whose input is nearest the cluster's mean consensus point; its image is the cluster's converged scene. The members' spread history is then cleared, so a cluster may converge again later (REP_NORELOC=1: the particles stay where they are).

The report (METRIC_EMIT=1). The converged scene is tested by the component metric of Section 5.3 against the $6$ nearest earlier converged scenes within $12\,$cm (4 join attempts each, alternating direction; MET_SEG=2.5e-4 MET_K=6 MET_ATT=4 MET_R=0.12). If a join succeeds it lies in the same component as an earlier scene and is not reported; otherwise it is reported as a new scene. Every converged scene, reported or not, enters the basin pull-back of Section 6.3.

10. How the results are scored

The score is a diagnostic made after the run; the solver never sees it. Each run's converged scenes (reported and not reported) are merged into a reference of about 21,400 flat states sampled independently of the solver (10,386 flat landings of the landing law and Majid's stable poses), already grouped into components by the same checked joins at $0.25\,$mm and used only as stepping stones. The first merge pass gives every converged scene a component label. A reported scene is a first find when no earlier reported scene has its label; the distinct share is first finds over reported scenes. A component is lost when the run converged in it but the component metric judged every scene there the same as an earlier one. Two scenes in different components are separated at the planner's resolution; separation is not proved.

11. Open points

  • Giant components. Three components hold about 70% of the reference's flat states. Most of the remaining repeats fall in them: their converged scenes lie far apart, beyond the component metric's 12 cm reach. Joins through a denser set of flat states (the solver's own landing-law samples, joined over several passes) connect them.
  • The swarm is mostly off the flat set. The local floor sets a stationary spread equal to the spacing of the flat arrivals, which is wide compared with the thin input regions that land flat. Over 300 iterations only 8-11% of the flat particles are still flat after a step, the noise is about four times the drift (0.09-0.11 against 0.025-0.027 per step, normalised inputs), and the consensus spread changes by a factor of about 0.99 per step rather than contracting at $\rho = 0.73$. Clusters therefore converge as measures over many basins, and the reported scene is the flat landing nearest their consensus point.
  • The metric is used at the report only. It decides which converged scenes are reported; it does not enter the consensus kernel, so a consensus measure can still couple particles of different components.
  • Lost components. About 10% of the components the swarm converged in were judged the same as an earlier scene and never reported. Confirming each deciding join at $0.02\,$mm, finer than the narrowest gaps between components, would remove false joins at small cost.
  • Separation. Two scenes in different components are separated at the planner's resolution; no certificate proves them separate.
  • Model. With $\mu = 10$ the friction cones have a half-angle of about 84°, and the mug's wall is 24 flat boxes, so a few equilibria (a spoon held by its shaft tip in a crease between two wall facets) exist only in the model.
