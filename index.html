Results

A scene is a connected component of the set of strongly stable (flat) equilibria: two configurations belong to the same scene when a path of equilibria joins them. The solver (Formulation) is PCBO with 1,000 particles that move only by the consensus SDE. When a cluster of particles converges, a path test on the stable configurations (checked paths of equilibria) decides whether its scene lies in a component already reported, and reports it only if not. Over 300 iterations on three seeds it reported 127, 137 and 171 scenes, of which 102, 111 and 130 are distinct components (80.3%, 81.0%, 76.0%; 78.9% pooled). After the first 100 iterations the share holds at about 81% per block of 50 iterations, and the count of distinct scenes keeps growing.

Formulation

The solver is a polarised consensus-based optimiser (PCBO; Fornasier and Sun, arXiv 2403.06662) on a manifold of drop poses. Its consensus points are refined by a balanced energy distance (Fornasier and Hütter, arXiv 1310.1120), its weights carry the nearest-neighbour density correction of MASEM (Braun, Burghoff and Toussaint, arXiv 2605.12338), and a path test on the set of stable configurations decides which converged consensus points it reports.

1. Configuration space

A configuration of rigid bodies $B \in \mathcal{B}$ on a horizontal support under gravity is

$$ x = (p_B, q_B)_{B \in \mathcal{B}} \in X = (\mathbb{R}^3 \times S^3)^{\mathcal{B}} , \tag{1} $$

$p_B$ the position of the body's origin and $q_B$ a unit quaternion ($\pm q$ identified). Gravity and the support are invariant under the group $G = SE(2)$ of horizontal translations and rotations about the vertical, acting on all bodies at once; the physically distinct configurations form $X/G$.

Twists and the retraction of $X$. A twist is $u = (v_B, L\,w_B)_{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{6|\mathcal{B}|}$, with $v_B$ a velocity of the body's origin, $w_B$ an angular velocity in the world frame and $L$ a length per radian. The configuration moved by $u$ is

$$ R^X_x(u) = \big(p_B + v_B,\ \exp(\tfrac12 w_B)\, q_B\big)_{B \in \mathcal{B}} , \tag{2} $$

the exponential map of each body's rotation group composed with a translation. The gauge directions at $x$ are the twists generated by $G$.

Alignment and interpolation. For $a, b \in X$, $g_{ab} \in G$ is the rotation about the vertical that best aligns a reference body of $b$ with that of $a$, followed by the horizontal translation that makes their positions coincide. The interpolation from $a$ to $b$ is

$$ \gamma_{ab}(s) = \big((1-s)\,p^a_B + s\, p^{g_{ab}b}_B,\ \operatorname{slerp}(q^a_B, q^{g_{ab}b}_B; s)\big)_{B}, \qquad s \in [0,1], \tag{3} $$

and the gauge-free distance is

$$ \begin{gathered} d(a,b) = \Big( \sum_{B} \|p_B^a - p_B^{g_{ab} b}\|^2 + L^2\, \theta(q_B^a, q_B^{g_{ab} b})^2 \Big)^{1/2}, \\ \theta(q,q') = 2\arccos|\langle q, q'\rangle| \end{gathered} \tag{4} $$

2. Contact strata

Features. $g_j : X \to \mathbb{R}$, $j = 1,\dots,F$, each $\ge 0$ off contact and $0$ in contact: signed distances between pairs of geometric parts (body and support, body and body), and point gaps that sample a part's faces and edges, so that a face lying flush is several point gaps at $0$.

Active and held features. Feature $j$ is active at $x$ when $g_j(x) \le b$, for a contact band $b$. The held set $H(x)$ is the active set less every pair gap equal to the least active point gap of the same pair (the pair is then in a face or line contact, which its point gaps imply).

Stratum. For a set $H$ of features,

$$ \Sigma_H = \{x \in X :\ g_j(x) = 0 \ (j \in H),\ \ g_j(x) > 0 \ (j \notin H)\} , \tag{5} $$

locally a manifold, with tangent space at $x$ (twists orthogonal to the gauge directions)

$$ T_x\Sigma_H = \{u \in \mathbb{R}^{6|\mathcal{B}|} :\ \nabla g_j(x)\cdot u = 0\ (j \in H),\ u \perp \text{gauge}\} , \tag{6} $$

where $\nabla g_j(x)\cdot u = \frac{d}{d\epsilon} g_j(R^X_x(\epsilon u))|_{\epsilon=0}$ with $R^X$ from (2). The rank $r_H(x)$ is the rank of the held gradients, each scaled to unit length, together with the gauge directions, less the number of gauge directions; $\dim(\Sigma_H/G) = 6|\mathcal{B}| - 3 - r_H(x)$.

Projection. For targets $\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{H}$, $\Pi_H(x;\tau)$ is the configuration with $g_j = \tau_j$ ($j \in H$) reached from $x$ by damped Gauss–Newton with minimum-norm steps $x \leftarrow R^X_x(u)$ (2), on a maximal independent subset of $H$ chosen greedily on the unit gradients (a row is kept when its component outside the span of the rows kept exceeds $\theta_p$), followed by a check that the remaining features of $H$ meet their targets, else on all of $H$. $\Pi_H(x) = \Pi_H(x; 0)$.

Retraction onto a stratum. For $x \in \Sigma_{H(x)}$ and $u \in T_x\Sigma_{H(x)}$ (6),

$$ R^\Sigma_x(u) = \Pi_{H(x)}\big(R^X_x(u)\big) . \tag{7} $$

3. Equilibria and scenes

At $x$ with active contacts $c = 1,\dots,n_c$ (normals $n_c$, friction coefficient $\mu$), each circular friction cone is replaced by the inscribed polygonal cone with $K_c$ generators $f_{c,k}$. The equilibrium residual is

$$ V_{\text{in}}(x) = \min_{\lambda \ge 0}\ \Big\| \sum_{c,k} \lambda_{c,k}\, W_{c,k}(x) - w_g \Big\| , \tag{8} $$

$W_{c,k}$ the wrench of $f_{c,k}$ on all bodies and $w_g$ the wrench that balances gravity. Because the cones are inscribed, $V_{\text{in}} = 0$ certifies an equilibrium.

Valid and exact. $x$ is valid when $g_j(x) \ge -\epsilon_{\text{pen}}$ for all $j$ and the bodies are in mutual contact; exact when it is valid and $V_{\text{in}}(x) \le \epsilon_{\text{eq}}$ (8).

Flat. For an exact $x$ and unit $u_1,\dots,u_{K_f}$ drawn uniformly in $T_x\Sigma_{H(x)}$ (6),

$$ \Phi(x) = \frac{1}{K_f}\, \#\{k :\ R^\Sigma_x(\rho_f u_k) \text{ is exact}\}, \qquad \mathcal{F}_{\text{flat}} = \{x \text{ exact} :\ \Phi(x) \ge \tfrac12\} , \tag{9} $$

with $R^\Sigma$ from (7); the steps are drawn in a fixed order and the count stops once the majority is decided.

Scene. A connected component of $\mathcal{F}_{\text{flat}}/G$ (9): two flat configurations belong to the same scene when a path in $\mathcal{F}_{\text{flat}}$ joins them, up to $G$. A path may cross several strata (5).

4. Input space and landing map

Input space. $Z = SO(3)^{\mathcal{B}} \times Q$, one orientation per body and a box $Q$ of positions and heights (the reference body's horizontal position fixed at the origin, which removes the translational gauge). In coordinates normalised by fixed scales (rotation vectors by $\pi$, box coordinates by the box's side lengths $w$),

$$ \begin{gathered} \operatorname{Log}_z(z') = \Big( \tfrac{1}{\pi} \log\big(q'_B q_B^{-1}\big),\ (y' - y) \oslash w \Big) \in \mathbb{R}^{\dim Z}, \\ |z' \ominus z| = \|\operatorname{Log}_z(z')\| \end{gathered} \tag{10} $$

$\log$ the rotation vector and $y$ the box coordinates of $z$. The retraction of $Z$ is

$$ R^Z_z(s) = \Big( \exp\big(\tfrac{\pi}{2} s_{B}\big)\, q_B,\ \operatorname{refl}_Q\big(y + w \odot s_Q\big) \Big), \qquad s \in \mathbb{R}^{\dim Z}, \tag{11} $$

left multiplication by the exponential on each orientation and reflection at the faces of $Q$; $R^Z_z(\operatorname{Log}_z(z')) = z'$ for $z'$ near $z$ (10).

Landing map. $x_0 : Z \to X$ places the bodies as $z$ prescribes. For a target rank $r$, the held set $H_r(z)$ takes the features in increasing order of $g_j(x_0(z))$ and keeps each whose gradient raises the rank of the kept gradients with the gauge directions (by a component above $\theta_r$), until rank $r$, every penetrating feature included. Then

$$ \Lambda_r(z) = \Pi_{H_r(z)}\big(x_0(z)\big) \tag{12} $$

with $\Pi$ from Section 2; features that penetrate after the projection join $H_r(z)$ and the projection is repeated, at most $n_{\text{proj}}$ times, after which the landing fails ($\Lambda_r(z) = \varnothing$). $\Lambda_r$ is piecewise smooth and many-to-one.

Objective.

$$ E(z) = \begin{cases} \min\{V_{\text{in}}(\Lambda_r(z)),\ E_{\max}\} & \Lambda_r(z) \text{ valid},\\ E_{\max} & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \tag{13} $$

with $V_{\text{in}}$ from (8). Every flat configuration is a global minimiser.

5. The swarm and its records

The swarm is $z_1,\dots,z_N \in Z$ (Section 4); particle $i$ has a fixed target rank $r_i$, the ranks cycling over the particles. At iteration $t$ its image is $x_i^t = \Lambda_{r_i}(z_i^t)$ (12) (the unlanded pose $x_0(z_i^t)$ if the landing fails), and its flat verdict is $f_i^t = 1$ when $x_i^t \in \mathcal{F}_{\text{flat}}$ (9); a particle whose image is exact and holds the same set as its flat image at $t-1$ keeps $f_i^t = 1$. The run records:

  • the evaluations $\mathcal{Q}_t = \{(x_i^s, f_i^s) : s \le t,\ x_i^s \text{ valid}\}$;
  • the flat arrivals $A_t = \{x_i^s :\ s \le t,\ f_i^s = 1,\ f_i^{s-1} = 0\}$, with $n_t = |A_t|$, and their inputs $Z^{\text{flat}}_t = \{z_i^s : x_i^s \in A_t\}$;
  • the converged scenes $Y_t \subset X$ of the iterations before $t$, each marked reported or not ($Y_1 = \varnothing$; $Y_{t+1}$ is given by (30)), and for each $y \in Y_t$ the iteration $t_y$ at which it converged.

6. Distances and density estimates

Placement metric. With $c_B$ the centre of mass and $\Sigma_B$ the second moment of body $B$'s mass distribution,

$$ \begin{gathered} d_X(a,b)^2 = \sum_B \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|} \Big(\lvert c^a_B - c^b_B\rvert^2 + \operatorname{tr}\big((R^a_B - R^b_B)\,\Sigma_B\,(R^a_B - R^b_B)^\top\big)\Big), \\ d_G(a,b) = \min_{g\in G} d_X(a, g b) \end{gathered} \tag{14} $$

the root-mean-square displacement of the bodies' matter, minimised over $G$ in closed form (planar Procrustes); $d_G$ is a metric on $X/G$.

Contact term. With the held sets $H$ of Section 2 and $\lambda_c$ the bodies' mean RMS radius,

$$ \delta(a,b)^2 = d_G(a,b)^2 + \lambda_c^2\,\lvert H(a) \,\triangle\, H(b)\rvert . \tag{15} $$

Graph distance on the swarm. $D_{ij}$ is the shortest-path distance between $x_i^t$ and $x_j^t$ in the $k_g$-nearest-neighbour graph of the images $\{x_i^t\}$ (Section 5) with edge lengths $\delta(x_i^t, x_j^t)/s$ (15) (Bernstein, de Silva, Langford and Tenenbaum).

Nearest-neighbour radius. Following MASEM, on the flat arrivals $A_t$ (Section 5),

$$ \begin{gathered} k_t = \lceil \sqrt{n_t}\, \rceil, \\ \varepsilon_t(x) = \text{the } k_t\text{-th smallest of } \{ d_G(x,a) : a \in A_t,\ d_G(x,a) > 0\} \end{gathered} \tag{16} $$

with $d_G$ from (14). Since $k_t \to \infty$ and $k_t/n_t \to 0$, the density estimate on the $p$-dimensional flat set

$$ \hat\rho_t(x) = \frac{k_t}{n_t\, \omega_p\, \varepsilon_t(x)^{p}} , \qquad \omega_p \text{ the volume of the unit } p\text{-ball}, \tag{17} $$

is consistent (Loftsgaarden and Quesenberry).

Scale and dimension. On a random subsample $S_t \subseteq A_t$, with $T_j(a)$ the distance (14) from $a$ to its $j$-th nearest other arrival,

$$ r_t = \operatorname{median}_{a \in S_t} \varepsilon_t(a), \qquad p_t = \Big( \frac{1}{|S_t|} \sum_{a\in S_t} \frac{1}{k_t - 1} \sum_{j=1}^{k_t-1} \log \frac{T_{k_t}(a)}{T_j(a)} \Big)^{-1} , \tag{18} $$

the median radius (16) and the intrinsic dimension (Levina and Bickel's maximum-likelihood estimate, its inverse averaged over points as MacKay and Ghahramani propose).

Visit potential. The log density (17) at $x$ relative to the density at the median radius (18):

$$ V_t(x) = \log\frac{\hat\rho_t(x)}{\hat\rho_t|_{\varepsilon_t = r_t}} = p_t \log \frac{r_t}{\varepsilon_t(x)} . \tag{19} $$

Flat-set classifier. With the evaluations $\mathcal{Q}_t$ (Section 5) and $\kappa_t = \lceil\sqrt{|\mathcal{Q}_t|}\,\rceil$,

$$ \phi_t(x) = \frac{1}{\kappa_t}\, \#\{(x', f') \in \mathcal{Q}_t \text{ among the } \kappa_t \text{ nearest to } x \text{ in } d_G :\ f' = 1\} . \tag{20} $$

7. Consensus points

Weights. Particle $i$ weights particle $j$ by

$$ \omega_{ij} \propto \exp\big(-\alpha_i D_{ij}^2\big)\, \exp\big(-\tfrac12 \bar V_j\big), \qquad \bar V_j = \begin{cases} V_t(x_j^t) & f_j^t = 1,\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \tag{21} $$

with $D$ from Section 6 and $V_t$ from (19); the second factor is MASEM's distance-power weight $(\varepsilon_t/r_t)^{p_t/2}$. $\alpha_i$ is set by bisection so that $(\sum_j \omega_{ij})^2 / \sum_j \omega_{ij}^2 = n_{\text{eff}}$.

Consensus point. One step of the weighted Fréchet mean on $Z$, with $\operatorname{Log}$ and $R^Z$ from (10) and (11):

$$ m_i = R^Z_{z_i}\Big(\sum_j \omega_{ij}\, \operatorname{Log}_{z_i}(z_j)\Big) . \tag{22} $$

Representatives and affinity. The representative $x^{\text{rep}}_i$ of consensus point $i$ is the image of the flat particle $j$ with $\omega_{ij} > w_{\min}$ (21) nearest $m_i$ (22) in $|\cdot \ominus \cdot|$ (10). For two consensus points with representatives $a = x^{\text{rep}}_i$, $b = x^{\text{rep}}_j$, the stratum separation is

$$ \sigma_{ij} = \max\Big(0,\ r_{H(a)}(a) + r_{H(b)}(b) - 2\max\big(r_{H(a)\cap H(b)}(a),\ r_{H(a)\cap H(b)}(b)\big)\Big) \tag{23} $$

($0$ when $H(a) = H(b)$; ranks from Section 2), and the affinity, with $\phi_t$ from (20) and the interpolation $\gamma_{ab}$ of (3),

$$ a_{ij} = \frac{\min_{s \in \{1,\dots,9\}} \phi_t\big(\gamma_{ab}(s/10)\big)}{1 + \max(0,\ \sigma_{ij} - 1)} . \tag{24} $$

Pull-back set. The inputs of flat arrivals that landed near a converged scene (Section 5):

$$ P_t = \{ z \in Z^{\text{flat}}_t :\ d_G\big(x(z), y\big) \le \varepsilon_{t_y}(y) \text{ for some } y \in Y_t \}, \tag{25} $$

$x(z)$ the arrival landed from $z$ and $\varepsilon$ from (16).

Repelled consensus points. With the energy-distance exponent $q \in [1,2)$, the target $\omega_t$ (the law of $R^Z_{z_a}(\tau\, \operatorname{Log}_{z_a}(z_b))$, $\tau \sim U[0,1]$ and $z_a, z_b$ drawn from $Z^{\text{flat}}_t$ with weight proportional to the $m_\omega$-th power of the distance (14) from their arrival to the nearest other arrival), the $K_r$ consensus points $N_i$ nearest $m_i$ whose representative differs from $x^{\text{rep}}_i$, and the $K_b$ points $P_t(c)$ of $P_t$ (25) nearest $c$,

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{B}_i(c) &= |c \ominus m_i|^2 + \mu_t \Big[ \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\omega_t}|c \ominus y|^{q} - \frac{1}{|N_i|}\sum_{j \in N_i} a_{ij}\,|c \ominus c_j|^{q} \\ &\qquad\qquad - \frac{1}{K_b}\sum_{y \in P_t(c)} |c \ominus y|^{q} \Big] , \\ \mu_t &= \beta\, \operatorname{median}_{i\ne j} |m_i \ominus m_j|^{2-q} \end{aligned} \tag{26} $$

a balanced energy distance (attraction to the target, repulsion from the other consensus points weighted by $a_{ij}$ (24) and from the pull-back set), with $|\cdot\ominus\cdot|$ from (10) and $m_i$ from (22). Starting from $c_i = m_i$, the consensus points take $n_B$ Riemannian gradient steps $c_i \leftarrow R^Z_{c_i}\big(\operatorname{clip}_{c_B}(-\eta\, \operatorname{grad} \mathcal{B}_i(c_i))\big)$ (11), jointly (Jacobi), with $\operatorname{grad}_c |c \ominus y|^{q} = -q\,|c\ominus y|^{q-2} \operatorname{Log}_c(y)$.

8. The dynamics

Write $\ell_i = |c_i \ominus z_i|$ and $v_i = \operatorname{Log}_{z_i}(c_i)$ (10), $c_i$ the repelled consensus point of (26). Each particle follows the stochastic differential equation on $Z$

$$ dz_i = \lambda\, v_i\, dt + \sigma\, \operatorname{diag}\big(|v_{i,k}| + \kappa_i\big)_{k=1}^{\dim Z}\, dW_i , \tag{27} $$

written in the coordinates of $T_{z_i}Z$ given by (10): drift towards the particle's own repelled consensus point and anisotropic noise (Carrillo et al., 2019) with the floor

$$ \kappa_i = \frac{\varepsilon_i}{s_z} \sqrt{\frac{1-\rho}{\dim Z\; \sigma^2 \Delta t}}, \qquad \rho = (1-\lambda\Delta t)^2 + \sigma^2\Delta t , \tag{28} $$

where $\varepsilon_i = \varepsilon_t(x_i^t)$ (16) if $f_i^t = 1$ and $r_t$ (18) otherwise, and $s_z$ converts lengths to input coordinates; it makes the stationary spread of a consensus measure equal to the local spacing of the flat arrivals. The equation is integrated by the Euler–Maruyama scheme in the retraction (11):

$$ z_i \leftarrow R^Z_{z_i}\Big(\operatorname{clip}_{c_s}\big(\lambda \Delta t\, v_i + \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}\, (|v_{i,k}| + \kappa_i)_k \odot \xi_i\big)\Big), \qquad \xi_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) . \tag{29} $$

9. Convergence and the report

Spread and stall. The spread of particle $i$'s consensus measure is $V_i = \sum_j \omega_{ij}\,|c_i \ominus z_j|^2$, with $\omega$ from (21) and $c_i$ from (26). With the relaxation time $\tau = \lceil 1/(-\ln\rho)\rceil$, $\rho$ from (28), particle $i$ has stalled when the mean of $V_i$ over its last $\tau$ iterations is not below its mean over the $\tau$ iterations before.

Clusters. Stalled particles $a, b$ are linked when $|c_a \ominus c_b|^2 \le \max(V_a, V_b)$; the clusters are the connected groups (single linkage). A cluster's converged scene is the image of the flat particle among its members and their supports ($\omega_{ij} > w_{\min}$) whose input is nearest the cluster's mean consensus point. The members' spread histories are then cleared; the particles stay where they are.

Component test. Two flat configurations lie in the same scene when a path in $\mathcal{F}_{\text{flat}}$ joins them. The solver tests a pair $a, b$ by a checked join at resolution $h$. The segment from $a$ to $b$ passes when every point $\Pi_{H}\big(\gamma_{ab}(s);\ (1-s)\,g_H(a) + s\, g_H(b)\big)$, $H = H(a) \cap H(b)$, $s$ spaced so that consecutive points are at most $h$ apart in $d$ (4), is exact ((3), projection, exact). The join is the segment when $d(a,b) \le R_s$; otherwise a planner repeats from $x = a$: among the step of length at most $H_p$ towards $b$ within $T_x\Sigma_{H(x)}$ (6), retracted by (7), and the same step after releasing one held contact that $b$ does not hold, it takes the one ending nearest $b$ if it gets closer, else a random step in the stratum; a step is accepted when its end is exact and the segment to it passes. It succeeds when the segment from $x$ to $b$ passes, and gives up after $n_{\text{stall}}$ steps without progress. A success shows that $a$ and $b$ lie in one scene at resolution $h$; a failure shows nothing.

Report. A converged scene $x$ at iteration $t$ is tested against its $K_m$ nearest scenes $y \in Y_t$ (Section 5) with $d(x,y) \le R_m$ (4), by up to $A_m$ checked joins each in alternating directions. It is reported when no join succeeds. Then

$$ Y_{t+1} = Y_t \cup \{\text{the converged scenes of iteration } t\}, \tag{30} $$

each marked reported or not, with $t_y = t$. The output of the solver is the set of reported scenes.

10. The instance and the run

Instance. Two bodies, a mug and a spoon, on a table, friction coefficient $\mu = 10$. The mug's wall is 24 boxes, with a base and a handle of capsules. The $F = 413$ features are the signed distances between the table and every mug and spoon geom and between every mug geom and both spoon geoms, plus point gaps: the 8 corners of every wall box and 8 points of the base's bottom rim against the table, both ends of every handle capsule against the table, and both ends of the spoon's shaft against the table, every wall box, the base and every handle capsule. The input space is $Z = SO(3) \times SO(3) \times Q$ with $Q = [0, 0.02] \times [-0.2, 0.2]^2 \times [0, 0.2]$ (metres): the mug's and the spoon's orientations, the height of the mug's lowest point above the table and the spoon's origin, the mug centred at the origin. Its scales $w$ are $0.02\,$m for the height and $0.4\,$m for the spoon's position. Wrenches are in units of $0.3 \cdot 9.81\,$N and $0.1\,$m. Gaps between neighbouring scenes can be as narrow as $20$-$100\,\mu$m.

11. Open points

  • Giant scenes. Three scenes hold about 70% of the flat configurations of an independent reference sample. Most repeats fall in them: their converged scenes lie far apart, beyond $R_m$, so the checked joins of the report do not reach them.
  • The swarm is mostly off the flat set. The floor $\kappa_i$ of (28) makes the stationary spread equal to the spacing of the flat arrivals, which is wide compared with the thin regions of $Z$ that land flat. Over 300 iterations only 8-11% of the flat particles are still flat after a step, the noise is about four times the drift, and the spread $V_i$ changes by a factor of about 0.99 per iteration rather than $\rho$.
  • The component test acts at the report only. The weights (21) use $D$, not the test, so a consensus measure can couple particles of different scenes.
  • Lost scenes. About 10% of the scenes the swarm converged in were judged the same as an earlier one by a checked join and never reported. A second check of each deciding join at a resolution below the narrowest gaps would remove false joins.
  • Model. With $\mu = 10$ the friction cones have a half-angle of about 84°, and the mug's wall is 24 flat boxes, so a few equilibria (a spoon held by its shaft tip in a crease between two wall facets) exist only in the model.
