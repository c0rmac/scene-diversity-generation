Each sample is one draw of the starting law: a point on the mug, a point on the spoon, a turn and a direction of gravity. The scene shown first is the draw itself. Moving the slider keeps the two points and turns the spoon about the common normal in 48 steps. The strip marks the turns at which the spoon can touch the mug there without overlapping it elsewhere (filled) and those at which it cannot (hatched). At a blocked turn the map still returns a scene: the spoon is pushed out along the mug's normal until nothing overlaps, so it touches the mug somewhere else.

Sampled from chooses the region in which the mug's contact place is drawn: its whole surface, or only the part that faces the inside of the cup. Sampled contact points marks where the contact places of 2,500 draws of the chosen region fall (blue on the mug, orange on the spoon). Point clouds shows the clouds themselves, thinned, in grey: the two are different things, and the floor of the cup is covered by the cloud although almost no contact falls on it.

Mug chooses how the mug stands. In any orientation: the direction of gravity is sampled freely and the pair is set on the table by its lowest point. Resting, any stable posture: the mug takes one of its own resting postures, upright, upside down, or on its side with the handle down either way. Resting upright: the mug stands upright in every scene. In the last two the mug is set on the table by itself, and a turn is free only if the spoon touches the mug at the chosen points and also stays above the table; at a turn where the spoon would pass through the table it is raised straight up until it is clear.
